# Rules notes and interpretations

These are the places where the rules are silent or ambiguous, or where the components disagree with the Living Rules, and what the app does. Each has a rule reference. Where an option exists, it is on the setup screen.

## Sources and conflicts
1. **Victory levels** follow the Living Rules 3.3.2 (−33 / −22 / −11 / ±10 / +11 / +22 / +33). The Player Aid VIII image in the VASSAL module still shows the older ±53 / ±32 / ±11 bands.
2. **Cutting-Out (optional OA2)** allows one Deployment per side per game, as in the Living Rules (A7) and the Playbook. Some Target Cards still say “maximum two”.
3. **A.I. American attack precondition (British campaign).** The British Living Rules LC1 text compares *Carronade + Defense* for the A.I. Americans. The Lake Combat Sequences of Play mat compares *Long Gun + Defense*, which matches the U.S. Breakout rule (14.3). The default is the mat; it can be switched on the setup screen.
4. **Hit Distribution Table** (Player Aid VI) is transcribed for 1–18 Engaged ships from the full table reproduced in the British Living Rulebook (p. 32); the U.S. rulebook's copy is cut off after column 12. Only Open Range can push the column past 18; then the position is picked uniformly. Hits on empty positions miss. The attacker's column in a Gunnery Round holds only the ships attacking in that Round: Reduced ships stay behind in the previous Round's zone and cannot be hit by return fire (Playbook p. 15).
5. **Adverse Wind** is applied as printed on all four cards: +2 to the human player's Reaction Checks in that zone. It reads like a misprint for −2 (Fair Wind gives the same +2), but the printed value is kept by player ruling (Nov 2026).

## Dice readings
6. The d10 is read **0–9** for the Lake Combat Table, Reaction Checks, the Convoy Scatter Table, Cutting-Out and Minor Damage. It is read **1–10** for Strike Checks (1–2 / 3–8 / 9–10), the Upgrade-limit roll (1 = 3 ships … 10 = 0) and the Close Action “roll ≤ CA difference” check. (The combat mat prints the Close Action check as “1d10 roll > ratio numerator hits”; the errata of 1 Dec 2023 says the mat is wrong and to follow LC7d, where a roll ≤ the difference hits.)

## Preparation Phase
7. **In Ordinary is reversible.** In A4 you choose which ships are laid up this turn, and previously laid-up ships can be recommissioned. The A.I. recommissions its laid-up ships each A4 and then applies the 30-point limit: Secondary ships go first, then the lowest capture VP, and never the Flagship. Ships placed In Ordinary in A5 to buy down Construction Delay count only in the turn they are placed.
8. **Flagships:** if a side's Flagship is not Deployable in A7, the highest capture-VP Deployable ship becomes Flagship (you can change yours in the A7 editor). The U.S. Flagship becomes the Pike automatically when she first becomes Deployable.
9. **British upgrades (British campaign 7.1/7.2):** the A2 roll limit applies to A-version ships in Deployable, and to those in Repairing that will finish repairs this turn. Captured Laker Schooners in the British In Transit area add +1 each and are then removed.
10. **U.S.-campaign Upgrade Checks (7.1)** are made only when British ships deploy for an Encounter, including the Homeport pool reacting and ships sortieing in a British Breakout Attack, using a 50/50 odd/even roll per A-version ship. Isolated ships never upgrade. Optional OA1 replaces this with one d10 roll per turn at the first Encounter.

## A.I. deployment
11. Deployment-table row = A.I. Troops in the Available box plus those embarked on A.I. Forces (capped at 3). Column = the Naval Balance band.
12. **Attack Convoy secondary missions.** In the U.S. campaign they trigger the Convoy procedure in both the Enemy Operations Phase (11.3.1 case 2) and a Lake-Action Encounter (as in the Playbook example, where your Escort Force can be found). In the British campaign they apply only in the Enemy Operations Phase, because British 9.1.2 says the Escort Convoy box Deploys only then; in a Lake-Action Encounter the card is a plain Patrol (player ruling, Nov 2026). In the Lake-Action case the Force that triggered the Encounter defends the Convoy along with your Escort Forces. If you have no Escort, the A.I. rolls on the Scatter Table and the Encounter ends.
13. An **Escort** card in a Lake-Action Encounter is a Patrol while the Sacket's Lake Zone holds a Blockade marker: both rulebooks' 9.1.2 allow Convoy Encounters only on turns when Sacket's is free of Blockade (the U.S. rulebook also names Sacket's, and the app follows it as printed). Otherwise it reveals the A.I. Convoy marker. If there is no Convoy, the card is a Patrol. If the deployment cell is blank, the Convoy is unescorted and your Force rolls on the Scatter Table (full-strength guns only). Otherwise it is an escorted-Convoy Encounter (Patrol Segment only) followed by a Scatter attack if the escort is beaten.
14. **Cutting-Out cards** deploy nothing when the optional rule is off.
15. The **British A.I. never uses Converted Laker Schooners** in Lake Combat, either attacking or defending.

## Encounters and Lake Combat
16. **Line of battle:** A.I. columns are Flagship first, then by capture VP, then Close Action. Your defending column uses the same order. When attacking you choose the ships and tows, and the default tows the strongest-gunned schooners.
17. **A.I. attack choice:** all full-strength ships attack. The A.I. Americans tow schooners with every “T” ship. If the attack fails its threshold because of untowed schooners (1.5:1), the A.I. retries without them at 1:1. The first attack of an A.I. Breakout is not subject to the preconditions, because the Breakout decision was already made.
18. **The untowed-schooner DRM roll** (1d6) is made every Gunnery Round of a U.S. attack. It is skipped in Harbor Encounters and under Sweep Up & Fire.
19. **Tactics** last for the current Combat Sequence; when the Weather Gage changes hands, a new sequence starts and new Tactics are drawn. One-shot Tactics (Surprise, First Shot, Rake, Escape, Pursuit, Defender Outmaneuver) are used once. Defender Rake rolls each Attacker hit on the 2-ship column.
20. **Minor Damage** is used automatically (for you or the A.I.) on the first hit the protected side takes in a Gunnery Round in that zone.
21. **Missed Signal:** the randomly chosen ship sits out the first combat round her side fights in that zone, then joins the end of her column.
22. **Leader casualties:** the Leader is captured only if the Flagship is captured in Melee, or Strikes/sinks while no other friendly ship remains Engaged. Otherwise a lost Flagship just shifts the flag to the best remaining ship. Gale or Explosion sinking the Flagship kills the Leader. You are offered a flag transfer when your Flagship is Reduced. The A.I. transfers only when it is attacking and the transfer keeps its Command DRM.
23. **Withdrawal:** the whole Force withdraws (LC5): its Engaged ships, ships hit by the W result that pass their Disengagement Check, and ships that Disengaged earlier in the Encounter. Ships that just failed their check, and ships that “Fight On”, stay Engaged. Coastal missions of withdrawing Forces abort, and their Troops return to the Available box in F7 – for the A.I. as well as for you (since 0.2.5), so they cannot embark again the same turn.
24. **Pursuit** cancels the Defender's withdrawal and starts a new Encounter (a new Weather Gage draw; the zone's Lake Event stays in force).
25. **Reaction Segment** happens when the Patrol Segment ends with both sides still having Engaged ships. Your homeport-to-zone distance counts the port as one zone (11.2.3). **Intelligence** (Event cards 29/31) gives +1 and cancels the negative distance DRM while keeping positive ones such as Fair Wind. It is drawn in the Coastal Action Phase and applies on the next turn (the card: “apply this Event next turn”), to the first Encounter that involves the enemy Flagship, which uses it up whether or not anyone makes a Reaction Check. A Pursuit counts as a new Encounter, so the pursued Encounter uses it up first. If no such Encounter happens, it lapses at the end of that turn.
26. **Struck Colors:** if exactly one side withdrew or was eliminated, its struck ships are captured and the other side's are rescued. Otherwise each struck ship rolls 1d6 (1–3 captured).
27. **Sickness** lowers only Close Action Strength. **Haze** removes Long Guns in the first Gunnery Round 1 fought in that zone each turn.
28. **Gale:** losses from the Gale (sunk or captured on the beach) score no VPs, because they have a non-combat cause.

## Coastal and Tally
29. **Heated Shot:** the damaged ship goes to In Ordinary in F7, to Repairing in the next A3, and is Deployable the turn after. **Requisition:** the ship is out of play for the whole next turn.
30. **Harbor Encounter** defenders are the Deployable and In Transit ships at the targeted port. Defenders that strike are not captured, and Reduced defenders move to In Transit.
31. **Lake Control** counts the Steps of full-strength warships (≥5) with no full-strength enemy warships in the zone.
32. **Convoys:** a Convoy that is delayed (Convoy Delay event, Blockade, or a passed Breakout) gives no advance that turn. At an unblocked F7, flip Delay to Arrived. That marker gives +2 in the next unblocked F5. A current convoy can run on schedule when the blockade lapses in F2.3 while an older marker still awaits its F7 flip. When the A.I. stays blockaded in Step D, its marker for the turn receives a Convoy Delay. **Unmaintained Blockade:** if no blockading ships are on station outside the blockaded Homeport in Step D, no Breakout is needed (14.3 note), the marker lapses in F2.3 and that side's Convoy runs on schedule (player ruling, Nov 2026).
33. **Dual Blockade sudden death (14.2)** triggers when the blockaded side does not meet the Breakout requirements: for you, enemy ships on station and at least one full-strength ship in your home port; for the A.I., the 14.3 comparison (Carronade for the British, Long Gun for the Americans, in port vs the blockaders).
34. **Naval Balance** excludes ships in a Blockaded original Homeport (14.1.2) but includes Deployable Isolated ships (14.1.1).

## Player Aids V and VII
35. **Force Package selection (5.3.1):** Main Ships are used first. If they alone can meet the requirement, the app uses the fewest Main Ships that do. Otherwise it takes all Main Ships plus the fewest Secondary Ships that cover the rest. Among equally small sets, it picks at random using the seeded dice. (The Playbook example draws Secondary Ships at random until the target is reached. The rule's "smallest number of ships" governs here, so the result can differ by one ship.)
36. **Package #1** needs at least one Main Ship. **Package #7** leaves out Secondary ships with neither Long Gun nor Close Action (Asp, Lady of the Lake).
37. **Package #8** needs both Forces. The 1st Force is the fewest Main Ships totaling 8 Close Action, with an Army Support mission and as many Troops as it can carry. The 2nd Force is the fewest remaining ships (Main first) totaling 22 Long Gun, on Patrol. If either can't be met, or no A.I. Troops are Available, nothing Deploys. In a Lake Action Encounter, 1d6 picks which Force fights: odd = Army Support, even = Patrol. Both stay on the map. In Enemy Operations both simply Deploy.
38. **Package #9** takes the 0-Close Action Secondary with the lowest Long Gun, random among ties. **Package #11** draws Secondary ships at random until their steps total at least 5.
39. **Land Battle Table misprint:** in the printed 0 Long Gun / 3 Troops column, rolls 62–63 have no result (Attacker Success 35-61, Attacker Major Success 64-65). The app reads Attacker Success as 35–63, following the 65 / 64 / 63 progression of that band. All 14 other columns cover 11–66 exactly.
40. **Land Battle column shifts** (More Defenders −2) stop at the 0 Long Gun column. More than 3 Troops markers use the 3-Troops column.
41. **Stolen Supplies:** Player Aid VII says a successful Land Battle against a Homeport *advances the attacker's* Convoy Supply marker two spaces. The Living Rules F5.3 (2024) say the *defender's* marker *regresses* two spaces, and the errata (1 Oct 2023) deletes the player-aid paragraph. The app regresses the defender.

## Not automated
42. Optional Advanced rules beyond OA1 and OA2 are not implemented.

## Added in 0.2.1 (audit of 0.2.0)
43. **Close Action hit then Melee (LC7d):** a hit defender makes its Disengagement or Strike Check at once. If it is still Engaged afterwards (failed check, or “Fight On!”), the same two ships go to a Melee, using their current Close Action strengths. This follows the LC5 sidebar and the Playbook example (Wolfe vs Lady of the Lake). Gen. Pike still never Melees.
44. **Close Action matchups (LC7b), your attack:** the app makes the largest possible number of 2:1 matchups, giving the weakest free defender to each attacker from the weakest up. The A.I. still chooses by LC7c (capture VP, then guns, then lowest CA).
45. **Commanding Breeze** affects only the Encounter it was drawn for (cards 37–38), not later Encounters in that zone the same turn.
46. **Last-turn sudden death (5.1)** counts an A.I. ship as eligible if it will be Deployable on Sept. II: Deployable or Make Ready; Repairing with no delay marker (not a newly built damaged ship); In Transit at full strength; laid up and undamaged (the A.I. recommissions it); or due back from Requisition at full strength. A Reduced ship returns via In Transit to Repairing and cannot avert the 5.1 ending.
47. **Blockaded A.I. ships in Lake Action** may Deploy for an Encounter in any zone (player ruling, Nov 2026); 14.1.2 bars them only from missions and the Enemy Operations Phase.
48. **Fast ships:** both rulebooks (LC5.2) and the errata (which answers exactly this question) say a full-strength “F” ship automatically passes her first Disengagement Check. The Playbook example has the Lady of the Lake roll; the app follows the rulebooks.
49. **Final-turn cards:** the British rulebook says 27 cards carry the final-turn symbol; 28 B-deck cards actually show it. The app removes the cards that show it.
50. **VP errata:** the errata's corrected Player Aid VIII text (“destroyed ±2 to ±32”) does not match the map's VP list; the app uses the map values.

## Added in 0.2.2 (independent audit of 0.2.1)
51. **Scattered Convoys** receive a Delay marker and give no advance that turn. An unblocked F7 flips it to Arrived. Score +2 at the following unblocked F5 (note 32, Living Playbook pp. 16–17). Destroyed and Captured Convoys are lost.
52. **Flag transfer (LC5.1)** is also allowed when the Reduced Flagship has just Disengaged, while she is still in the column before the gap closes (Playbook p. 19). A wounded Leader cannot transfer.
53. **Withdrawal for your Force** is offered after a W result even when all your ships in the Encounter have Disengaged; accepting it sends them In Transit with the rest of the Force.
54. **Troops** are whole markers: fractional entries are refused in A7, in Adjust and in loaded saves.
55. **Lineup preview** shows the first Round exactly as it will be fought, including a Missed Signal ship sitting out and the A.I. British leaving Converted Laker Schooners out of the defending line.
56. **Loading a save** checks it first: every field and type, known ships and boxes, step limits per ship, Convoy Supply −1 to 12, whole-number VP, CD and Troops, and that every step in the procedure stack exists. A damaged, cut-off or incomplete save is refused with a message and the current game, its autosave and Undo history are left untouched. A save that is accepted can still be undone: one Undo right after loading brings back the game you had open, with its own Undo history. Saves from earlier versions are accepted; fields added later are filled in, including the record of which Flagships are in a current Encounter.
57. **Force Package 8 split (open question):** the first Force is a random seeded choice among all the smallest sets of Main ships with at least 8 Close Action; the app does not look ahead to see whether the ships left can still form the 22-Long Gun second Force. So in the same pool one roll can leave too little Long Gun and the package fails, while another leaves enough. The rules (Player Aid V #8) do not say which first Force to prefer.

## Added in 0.2.4 (follow-up audit of 0.2.3)
58. **Troops are never lost with their ships.** Unlaunched Troops come back at F7 whatever happened to the Force that carried them – a Gale, a sinking or a capture included. Nothing in the rules removes Troops with their transports, and F7 returns every unlaunched marker. The Reset screen now says how many returned and how many you have Available.
59. **Confirmed Encounter, no enemy deployment:** when the spyglass confirms an Encounter but no A.I. ships Deploy (blank cell, unmet Force Package, nothing Deployable), your Force is marked Encounter Ended (EN1.1 in both rulebooks; British rulebook p. 13). A Cutting-Out mission then aborts (OA2). A false alarm does not end anything.
60. **A.I. Convoy with no escort:** if the Escort card's package cannot be filled (or the A.I. has no Deployable ships), the Convoy sails unescorted and your Force rolls on the Convoy Scatter Table with its full-strength guns, exactly as for a blank cell [11.3.1 case 1].
61. **Cutting-Out is offered in A7 only against a port with targets** – enemy ships in its Deployable or Make Ready area – so the once-per-game mission cannot be spent on an empty port. If the ships have left by the Coastal Action Phase it still aborts there.
62. **Pursuit (A05)** applies only if the Defender has ships eligible to withdraw once Hits, Disengagement and Strike Checks are done: ships that just failed a check stay Engaged, and if that is every Defender nothing withdraws, the card is not used and the Combat Sequence goes on. When Pursuit does apply, Disengaged ships (both sides) come back into the new Encounter with their damage; Struck, Sunk and Withdrawn ships do not.
63. **Towing** counts in a Gunnery Round only while both the towing ship and the schooner are attacking in that Round (LC2, LC4.2). A schooner whose towing ship has Disengaged, been Reduced or is sitting out a Missed Signal rolls as untowed.
64. **Shipyard Damage 8–9** adds 2 Construction Delay and flips the ships on the stocks only when there is more than one, as printed on the Homeport Display.
65. **Last Step / No Steps markers** are discarded when ships Withdraw and at F7: a Reduced ship goes back to the steps on its printed Reduced face (two for Royal George, Wolfe, Pike, Madison and Sylph; one for the two-step ships; none for the one-step ships).
66. **Rake (A06):** you see the random ship before choosing among lead, rear and random, and that choice is kept through reload and Undo. A used Attacker Rake goes back to its deck at once (Playbook p. 19). Other used or negated Tactics stay out until the decks are rebuilt at F7: the rules do not say when they return (open question).
67. **Command Decision odds** are worked out like the lineup preview and the first Round itself: same defenders, Missed Signal and Haze.

## Added in 0.2.5 (audit of 0.2.4)
68. **Struck Colors under Pursuit (project ruling, 18 Nov 2026):** when Pursuit cancels a DW withdrawal, the Encounter that just ended settles its Struck Colors ships at once, before the new Encounter draws its Event, as if the DW withdrawal had happened: the pursued (Defending) side's struck ships are Captured, the Attacker's are Rescued, and if the Attacker withdrew too (AW/DW) each one rolls 1d6 as in LC5.3. LC5.3 does not name this ending; the ruling keeps Pursuit from ever making a capture less likely, and nothing that happens in the new Encounter can change those fates.
69. **Aborted coastal missions:** the A.I.'s unlaunched Troops now wait for F7 like yours (note 23), instead of going straight back to its Available box. Board → Tracks shows how many Troops of each side are due back at F7.
70. **Saves made by 0.2.3 at the Rake choice:** on loading (file, pasted text, autosave or Undo) the random ship is rolled from the saved dice – the same ship 0.2.3 would have picked – and named on the button before you choose.

## Added in 0.2.6 (audit of 0.2.5)
71. **Saves made by 0.2.4 inside the two old windows are upgraded once on loading** (file, pasted text, autosave or Undo), and only on clear evidence in the current turn's log:
    * an A.I. withdrawal that put its Troops straight back in the Available box (L01): the markers move to the F7 queue. If the A.I. has already sailed or landed with Troops since, nothing is changed and a message asks for a save from before the withdrawal;
    * a ship that Struck Colors before a Pursuit and is still undecided (L02): it is settled at once by note 68, the pursued side taken from the last Gunnery Round before the Pursuit. Ships that struck in the new Encounter are left alone.
    A message on the screen says what was changed. Upgraded saves are marked so this happens only once.

## Added in 0.2.7 (audit of 0.2.6)
72. **Which saves are upgraded (replaces the detection in note 71).** Every save written by 0.2.7 or later carries a build stamp (`build`) and is never upgraded – its surrenders and Troops were handled by the corrected procedures. Unstamped saves (0.2.6 and earlier) are upgraded only on affirmative evidence:
    * **Old Pursuit surrender:** a Struck Colors ship counts as pre-Pursuit only if its Strike Check line comes before the last Pursuit line this turn, or if no combat has taken place since that Pursuit. A strike in the new Encounter – logged after the Pursuit or still in the pending combat lines – is left for the end of its own Encounter. The old ship is settled from the sides' status at the Pursuit boundary (the pursued Defender withdrawn, the pursuer present: the Defender's struck ships Captured, the Attacker's Rescued), so casualties in the next Encounter cannot change the result. If the save can't show which Encounter a surrender belongs to, or which side was pursued, nothing is changed and a notice asks for a save from before the Pursuit.
    * **Early A.I. Troops return:** the markers move to the F7 queue only if the A.I. has not counted them since – no deployment lookup on a Troops row of 1 or more, no deployment in progress with such a row, no Force sailing with Troops and no landing. Otherwise, and always at Tally, the save is left as saved and a notice asks for a save from before the withdrawal.

## Added in 0.2.8 (audit of 0.2.7)
73. **Recovery notices stay with the game.** When an older save can't be put right (note 72), its notice is stored on the board itself, shown above every prompt, and kept through autosave, export, reload and Undo until you load another game or choose "Keep playing this position". Repairs that succeed are still reported once.
    * **Saves already changed by 0.2.6** are recognised by contradictions the corrected procedures never produce, and flagged the same way (nothing is rolled back): a surrender settled in the middle of its own Encounter (its Strike Check is still pending or logged after the Pursuit), an old Pursuit surrender settled differently from note 68, or Troops moved to the F7 queue after the A.I. had already chosen a deployment row with them.
    * Saves written by 0.2.7 after one of its notices are checked once more for the notice only.
    * A state restored by Undo is stamped with the current build, like a loaded save.

## Added in 0.2.9 (fresh-game audit)
74. **Concurrent repair and upgrade [British 7.2]:** an A-version ship completing repairs with no delay can become B-version and Deployable immediately, within the current upgrade limit. An already Deployable ship selected for an ordinary refit still spends a turn in Make Ready [7.1]. The Living Playbook p. 12 demonstrates the concurrent case.
75. **Attacker Rake [A06]:** the selected target applies to its one Defender Hit result only. Later Gunnery Rounds and Weather Gage switches use normal Hit Distribution, even if the old target remains in the Encounter or has already sunk.
76. **Escape with END [D02, LC3c/g]:** add the card's DW to an END result before the Segment finishes. The human defender can accept or decline that withdrawal under LC5; either way, END still ends the Segment. Matching Pursuit/Escape color bars negate both as usual.
77. **A.I. attack composition [LC1, LC2]:** when a later Gunnery or Close Action Round meets the attack conditions only by omitting untowed schooners, that Round uses the selected ships and tows. Omitted ships remain Engaged for later defense, but do not fire or receive Gunnery return hits in that attack.
78. **Blockade Target Card instruction [A10]:** when a missing Homeport card is added from the deck or discards, the displayed layout count includes that extra card and explains why it was added. If a matching card was already drawn, moving it left adds no card.

**Open question:** mandatory A.I. Construction Delay buy-down [8.1] can conflict with keeping a Deployable Flagship [LC5.1]. The app preserves its current Flagship. Whether transferring the command should permit further buy-down needs a ruling; this audit did not change that policy.

79. **Command Decision A.I. odds (0.2.9).** The preview uses the same pure attack plan as the A.I. decision, including any omitted untowed schooners. It then removes Missed Signal from the first-round participants and applies Haze. A failed attack condition is displayed as a Pass. The human preview remains explicitly hypothetical: all full-strength ships.

80. **Missed Signal and A.I. conditions (0.2.9).** Remove the absent ship from the first Round's actual odds and tow participation. Keep LC1's separate comparison of printed weapon + Defense values on the Forces. An otherwise eligible ship uses up Missed Signal even if the player leaves it out of the attack.
81. **Possible Encounter choices [10.1.2/11.1.3] (0.2.9).** Choose each marker's recipient among the Forces with the fewest markers in that zone. A marker whose original Force left may transfer to any remaining friendly Force in the original zone; the transfer paragraph does not impose a least-markers restriction. Forced single-recipient assignments remain automatic.
82. **Failed deployment and OA1 (0.2.9).** An Army Support Force lacking capacity for one Troops marker cannot Deploy, so it does not make a 7.1/OA1 upgrade roll. OA1's post-upgrade lookup and displayed column both use the recalculated balance. Pending a designer ruling, the existing literal A6 recalculation still counts only currently Deployable ships; the alternative of adjusting the original A6 total only for upgrades is not silently substituted.
83. **Requisition [F7, 6/6.1] (0.2.9).** Requisitioned ships discard Step markers at F7 and retain their appropriate Homeport/Temporary Homeport. After the full following turn out of play, full-strength ships return to Deployable; Reduced ships return via In Transit and normal repair. The card does not name a special return box, so the normal damaged-ship repair cycle is used.
84. **Harbor Lake Zone effects [12] (0.2.9).** Pending Haze and Missed Signal apply in their first eligible Harbor Round: their cards' scope includes the zone's Lake Combat, and rule12 does not exclude them. Haze removes Long Guns once; Missed Signal excludes its ship from strengths and hits once. If that leaves one side empty for Round1, skip its resolution without dice and return the ship for Round2. A wholly unfought Round1 does not burn Haze; this narrow combined-event timing is an interpretation. Rule12's explicit two-Gunnery-Round procedure remains in force under Rainy Squalls, which otherwise changes a Patrol Segment to Close Action.
85. **Flag transfers [LC5.1] (0.2.9).** Offer the human the permitted adjacent full-strength transfer after Close Action too, and before replacing the old column when beginning a new attacking Combat Sequence/Reaction. Apply the required Leader Casualty Check; A.I. transfers only when beneficial under its existing policy.
86. **Preview and manual table descriptions (0.2.9).** Rainy Squalls Patrol previews show Close Action strengths and matchups; Reaction previews retain Gunnery. Manual Land Battle prompts name the final printed column after all shifts. A Harbor prompt uses the same eligible defender pool as the actual battle, including the A.I. British prohibition on Converted Laker Schooners.
87. **Pursuit and a human W choice — unresolved.** A05 explicitly triggers on a DW result and ships eligible to withdraw, while LC5 generally permits the human to decline W. The existing specific-card reading triggers Pursuit once eligibility is established, before a human withdrawal choice. The alternative would ask first and leave Pursuit unused if W is declined. The November2026 independent audits disagree; this release retains notes24/62/68 and does not claim the alternative is a confirmed defect.
88. **Other open interpretations.** The OA1 recalculation above, later-Encounter Rainy Squalls after a Reaction Segment, Defender Rake's optional timing, Tactic lifetime/recycling (notes19/66), Package8's first-Force choice, and the two unassigned Player AidVII results remain documented questions. The printed Construction Delay buy-down versus Deployable-Flagship requirements also need clarification. These are not silently changed by this release.

## 0.2.10 rules and table controls

89. **Gale and Separated Ships.** Event 30 requires a first Lake Zone Event in the Lake Action Phase. Discard it and redraw in other phases. Events 11, 14, 27 and 34 split a single qualifying Force. If several independent Forces qualify, choose one at random. Its displaced Patrol may attempt a normal Reaction. The card gives no priority among multiple qualifying Forces.
90. **Missed Signal and towing.** When the first Round would consist solely of a ship sitting out under Missed Signal on either side, the Round elapses without combat dice. The ship can return for the following Round. This resolves a timing gap on the card. A schooner counts as towed only while immediately behind its assigned tower (LC2).
91. **Weather Gage and flags.** Defender Outmaneuver D06 determines the winner of the next LC6 check. Draw the new Event for that check and use its symbols. LC5.1 flag transfers are resolved for both sides after hits, including Harbor combat under rule 12.
92. **Coastal sequencing and sudden death.** In E3/E6, launch at all eligible targets, resolve any Harbor Encounters, then fight the Land Battles. Resolve 14.2 at turn end, using the home fleet after F7 returns and the actual blockading force that was on station. Requisitioned ships count for the September 5.1 check only when their scheduled return makes them Deployable in time.
93. **Construction purchases.** After A5's free reduction, two Close Action points buy one whole Construction Delay reduction (8.1). Ships are committed only for an attainable purchase. The existing Flagship exclusion in note 88 still applies.
94. **Manual Force Packages.** The picker checks both parts of Aid V #8 before deployment and the printed priorities for the other packages. Suggestions preserve dice until the choice is committed. The existing first-Force selection policy under notes 35–38 applies, including a possible unsuccessful first selection.
95. **Tabletop pace.** Grouped updates is an optional presentation setting. It advances specified informational reports and plays a Tactic automatically when only one is legal. Its recap preserves the reports and public counter changes. Undo restores the entire preceding click, including its dice.

## 0.2.12 audit corrections and project rulings (19 November 2026)

96. **Melee Leader checks [LC5.1].** A Melee with a capture checks each participating Flagship's commander: +1 for the winner and +2 for the loser, plus the U.S. Grapple modifier when applicable. A losing commander who survives his check is captured with the ship. A commander killed by the check is not captured again. A no-result Melee makes no such check.
97. **Succession timing — project ruling.** Resolve the commander present at the start of a combat Round result. Per-hit checks continue while that commander is alive; once killed or captured, he takes no more checks, supplies no Command or original-Leader Strike modifier and cannot transfer the flag. Install his successor after the result, including its withdrawals and flag transfers, finishes. A Close Action hit and any ensuing Melee belong to the same Round result. A pending replacement is saved if a withdrawal or transfer decision interrupts resolution. The same timing applies in Harbor combat. This records the audit's recommended interpretation of an unstated timing boundary; it is not a new publisher ruling.
98. **Zero eligible guns — project ruling.** An unescorted Convoy attack requires at least one eligible gun. With none, do not roll on the Scatter Table and do not change the Convoy's result or supply arrival. Reduced ships contribute no guns under 11.3.1. The separate full-strength-attacker condition for an escorted Convoy in 11.3.2 still applies. This records the recommended treatment of the table's missing zero-gun row, not a publisher clarification.
99. **Manual corrections during an Encounter.** An Encounter participant's box, owner and steps are locked through every prompt until the Encounter finishes, including Command Decision, Tactics, round reports and lineup selection. Use Undo to before the Encounter if a correction is required. Unrelated ships and track corrections remain available. Pending selection and lineup safeguards still apply.

The earlier open interpretations in notes 87–88 remain open. These changes affect future resolutions. Loading an older position does not reroll casualties already resolved by an earlier release.
